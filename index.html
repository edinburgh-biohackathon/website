More than a hackathon

The Bio-Innovation Series

The weekend sits inside a longer programme. Participants train before it and keep building after it, with biologists, programmers and industry partners working side by side throughout.

  1. Before · weeks ahead

    Pre-hackathon workshops & training

    • Technical training: Git & GitHub, HPC on Eddie, Python
    • Intellectual property and commercialisation basics
    • Q&A sessions and team matchmaking

    2026: three workshops with Edinburgh Innovations, CIIE and Digital Research Services

  2. The weekend · 48 hours

    BioHackathon

    • Real challenges from academic labs and industry
    • Interdisciplinary teams of 4–6
    • Mentors on hand, final pitches to expert judges, prizes

    2026: 100+ participants, 7 challenge tracks, 15 judges & mentors

  3. After · following weeks

    Post-hackathon workshops & continuation

    • Entrepreneurship and commercialisation workshops
    • Mentoring to refine projects beyond the prototype
    • Routes to incubators, funding and collaborations

    2026: ran from the closing ceremony to late April

What the series is for

Objective

Build a cross-disciplinary innovation community

Unite life scientists and programmers through collaborative problem-solving on real biological and computational challenges.

Objective

Foster academia–industry partnerships

Co-design challenges with academic and industrial partners, exposing students to authentic problem-based learning and professional mentoring.

Objective

Create sustainable pathways for innovation

Post-event mentoring and support for project continuation, growing into a recurring Bio-Innovation Series.

Meet candidates while they build

Sponsorship 2027

A careers fair and a 48-hour assessment centre in one. See how people think, collaborate and build under pressure. Challenge providers get prototype solutions to real problems, and sponsors can have teams build on their APIs, software or compute for real developer feedback.

Academic Research Track

Data & confidentiality

No sensitive data required

Setting a challenge never needs live clinical, patient or confidential data. Typical sources:

  • Public repositoriesClinicalTrials.gov, UniProt, GEO, TCGA, PDB, Ensembl
  • Non-confidential subsetsFrom industry R&D or open-access labs
  • Synthetic or anonymised dataPrepared before the event
  • No data at allWorkflow, API testing or non-coding problems

Participants keep ownership of their code; challenge providers get non-exclusive access to all prototype repositories, documentation and methods.

FAQ

Quick answers

No coding needed. Non-coding tracks, pre-event workshops, and travel support up to £60.

Do participants need coding experience?

No. Some challenges involve coding, but dedicated non-coding tracks focus on experimental design, problem-solving and communication, so everyone can contribute.

How are teams formed?

Apply as an individual or as a team of 4–6. Individuals form teams on Discord and at in-person networking and team formation sessions before hacking starts.

Will there be prizes or certificates?

Yes. Winning teams receive prizes, and everyone gets a certificate of participation. Follow-on opportunities include incubators, funding and collaborations.

What support is available?

Pre-event workshops (technical skills, entrepreneurship, IP, Python, Git) and mentorship from academic and industry experts. Travel support covers partial to full reimbursement, up to £60 per person, based on competition and need.

How much time commitment is expected?

Workshops are optional; pick by skill level. The hackathon runs from Friday afternoon to Sunday afternoon, with final judging and awards on Sunday.

Can projects continue after the hackathon?

Yes. Selected projects may receive continued mentorship or sponsorship support, depending on partner interest and participant initiative.

Who organises the BioHackathon?

An independent, volunteer team of early-career researchers at the University of Edinburgh, led by Ian Yang (Swain Lab, School of Biological Sciences) and Apple Chew, with the Centre for Engineering Biology and partners across the University.

No confidential data needed. Bring a real problem; participants keep their code, you get access to every prototype.

What does it take to host a challenge?

Propose a real, non-confidential biological, computational or data problem (workflows, datasets, pipelines, conceptual challenges). Teams prototype solutions, analyses or tools over the weekend. See the sponsorship tiers for details.

What is expected from challenge providers?

Define the problem statement, context and success criteria. Production-ready software is not expected. You can support teams by:

  • Sharing docs, tutorials or example repos (e.g. in intro slides)
  • An optional workshop or Q&A (up to 1 hour)
  • Answering questions and giving domain guidance during the event

No continuous engineering support or debugging of team environments is expected. The organisers run best-effort pre-event sanity checks.

What compute is available?

The University of Edinburgh provides access to the Eddie HPC cluster (including GPUs), onboarding on compute resources, and OpenAI API access for LLM-enabled challenges.

What presence is expected?
  • Opening ceremony, Friday afternoon, to introduce your challenge
  • Judging, awards and closing, Sunday afternoon
  • Optional Saturday Q&As and mentoring
What do sponsors get?
  • A sponsor stall and structured networking with participants
  • Visibility across event materials
  • A 5–10 minute challenge introduction at the opening ceremony
  • The option to host a 1-hour workshop or technical session
Can sponsors deliver technical training?

Yes, e.g. Snakemake. Either in the pre-hackathon workshop series or on Day 1, alongside academic-led sessions such as Git and Python.

Do sponsors need to provide prizes?

Challenge partners are asked to contribute prizes for their track's winning team (typically 4–6 people): merchandise or digital products, internships, shadowing, fast-track interviews, or other relevant incentives.

Are sponsors expected to contribute financially?

Challenge Providers contribute prizes only; Bronze, Silver and Gold are paid tiers (see above). Covering your own staff's travel is expected.

Is it suitable for recruitment?

Yes. The event showcases technical ability, problem-solving, teamwork and communication. Bring both technical staff and talent representatives.

When and where is the 2027 event?

Spring / early summer 2027 at the University of Edinburgh. Exact dates are to be confirmed; sponsors are confirmed in early 2027.

How many participants will there be?

We had 100+ participants in 2026. For 2027 we are aiming for 200–300.

Can we sponsor without setting a challenge?

Yes. Any tier can set a challenge or opt out; just tell us your preference.

Who owns the code and IP?

Participants keep ownership of the code they write. Challenge providers get non-exclusive access to all prototype repositories, documentation and methods from their track.

Do we need to share confidential data?

No. All challenges are non-confidential. Public, synthetic or anonymised data works, or no data at all.

Can we give in-kind support instead of cash?

Yes: catering, compute credits, software licences or merchandise.

Is there a multi-year option?

Yes. Commit to two years for 10% off year two and credit as an Anchor Partner.

Do we get access to participant CVs?

Gold sponsors receive opt-in CVs and email contacts for all participants.

Each track brings together people from life sciences, computing and related fields. Teams compete in a final pitch session, and a judging panel picks the winners.

Academic Research

Open research problems from labs

Modelling gene regulation, analysing imaging data, drug discovery and design, or machine learning on biological datasets. Sample datasets, academic mentors, and a route to follow-up collaborations.

Industry Innovation

Practical, outcome-focused challenges from biotech, health and data companies, aimed at prototypes, pipelines or analytical insights. Direct exposure to industrial R&D and potential recruitment.

Participant guide

How it works

The rules and essentials from the 2026 participant info pack.

Who can take part

  • UG, PGT and PGR students, postdocs, academic staff, and recent graduates (within the past year) from UK universities
  • 18 or older
  • Registered and present in person for the weekend
  • Official volunteers can't compete as hackers

Teams of 4–6

Form your own team, or join one on Discord and at the Friday networking session. Final teams are set by the end of Day 1. Suggested mix:

Domain expert
AI engineer
Bioinformatics / data
Business / product / design

What to bring

  • Laptop and charger
  • Notebook and pen
  • Any software we tell you to install

We provide

  • Meals and refreshments, workspace
  • Mentors, facilitators and mental health breaks
  • Heat-and-eat stations, water points, baby feeding room; showers on request

Code rules

  • Core work happens during the event; no substantial pre-written project code
  • Public frameworks, libraries and tools are fine
  • AI tools (ChatGPT, Copilot, LLM APIs) allowed if disclosed and credited
  • Thin wrappers around existing tools may be disqualified

Submission · Sun 12:00

  • GitHub repository and a clear README (for coding projects)
  • List of frameworks, datasets and AI tools used
  • Pitch deck
  • Submitted on DevPost; late entries may not be judged

Judging

In person on Sunday: live demo, short pitch, judges' Q&A. Criteria vary by track and typically include:

Technical qualitySolution & value propositionCreativityRelevance to challengeFeasibilityPresentationTeam momentumBonus: extra mile

Intellectual property

  • Low-risk, exploratory event; you keep ownership of your ideas
  • No obligation to form a company
  • Organisers may share non-confidential summaries and images
  • Don't disclose confidential data, including your own PhD or postdoc research

Code of conduct

  • Be kind, collaborative and respectful of every background and skill level
  • No harassment or discrimination; follow venue safety rules
  • Credit all external code, data, models and tools
